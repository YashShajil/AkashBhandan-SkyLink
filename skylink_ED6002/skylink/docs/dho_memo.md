# SkyLink: recommendation for drone delivery of emergency medical supplies

**To:** District Health Officer, Kaveripuram  **From:** ED6002 project team  **Date:** November 2026

## The recommendation in one paragraph

Set up **five drone hubs** at SDH Nallur, SDH Vadakarai, SDH Melmalai, SDH Kadalpuram and PHC Arasampatti. Each hub gets **three drones**: 15 in total, with 7 charging points and 14 spare batteries. Every one of the 24 PHCs is served by a drone. On a normal day, blood or antivenom reaches a PHC in **about 15–20 minutes**, against 1.5–3 hours by road today. The system costs about **₹2.26 crore a year**, including staff, equipment, electricity and the road trips still needed in bad weather.

## What the district gets

| | Typical week | Average over a year | Worst monsoon week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergencies delivered within 45 minutes | **98%** | **94%** | **about 60%** |
| Orders that still go by road | 1–2% | about 7% | most daytime orders |

- **Weather is the limit, not the number of drones.** Drones cannot take off in strong gusts, which ground them in about 5% of all hours and in a quarter of all hours in July. Adding drones beyond 15 cost us up to ₹1.3 crore a year more in our tests and did not raise the on-time share.
- **The road service must stay.** An emergency that has waited 30 minutes for a drone goes by ambulance automatically. In the worst storm week of the year (late July, including a cyclone), 4 in 10 emergencies would go this way.
- **How sure are we?** We tested 300 random weeks in which demand was up to 25% higher or lower than expected and up to 1 drone in 10 was out of service. In **83%** of them, at least 90% of emergencies arrived within 45 minutes. The weeks that fell short were almost all monsoon weeks.

## What it costs and where the money goes

About **₹2.26 crore a year**. Roughly one third is **operators** (one per hub per shift, three shifts, 24×7). About one third is **drones and batteries** (each drone ~₹7.6 lakh, replaced every 3 years). Hubs (pad, shelter, generator, rent) are about 15%. The rest is electricity, battery wear and road trips in bad weather.

## The choices you can make

1. **Make the District Hospital (blood bank) a hub** instead of Melmalai: about **₹0.3 lakh a year more**. Coverage is the same and blood leaves straight from the blood bank. *We recommend this.*
2. **Four hubs with larger drones** (Nallur, Vadakarai, Kadalpuram, Arasampatti; 16 drones): about ₹3 lakh a year more. Road trips fall from about 7% to 5%. The on-time share for emergencies is about the same.
3. **Smaller, cheaper drones** need more hubs and still leave some PHCs on road delivery. The smallest drone we tested costs ₹3.3 crore a year and still left 4 PHCs on road delivery.
4. **Plan for growth.** If orders grow 15% a year, the recommended system still meets the 45-minute target for about 93% of emergencies in year 3. But road trips rise to about 11%. We advise reviewing the fleet after year 1. Year-2 demand is best served by about 24 drones and a sixth hub, at about ₹2.8 crore a year.
5. **Cheaper drones or better batteries in future** reduce cost: a 30% drop in drone price saves about ₹19 lakh a year, and batteries holding 20% more energy save about 5%.

## How we decided

We looked for the **cheapest system that delivers at least 90% of emergencies within 45 minutes in a typical week, and does so in at least 8 out of 10 random weeks.** Three finalists met the first condition; the recommended one was the cheapest and also the most reliable (83% of weeks). We also checked it against a calm week, a typical week and the worst week of the year. Changing the day of the week on which the planned power cuts fall made no difference.

## What could change these numbers

- The drone's rotor efficiency still uses **provisional figures**. The final test data from the rotor laboratory may move range and cost by a few percent. The hub locations are unlikely to change.
- The weather record is one year. A worse monsoon than this year's would lower the on-time share in July and August.
- We assumed every hub keeps a stock of blood and antivenom. Without that, only a District Hospital hub can send blood (choice 1).
